Editorial clarifications:
- Jarboe reports a household near a cryptocurrency mining campus, not a verified AI data center. Its contribution to bill changes is not isolated. The household's income is not inferred or assigned one of the fictional model incomes.
- IEA's 2030 demand value is a central projection, not a certain outcome.
- Masanet's historical global estimate and LBNL's newer US projection have different geography and time periods; they are not one continuous series.
- The 400 and 50 kg CO2e/MWh factors are illustrative assumptions, not claimed exact current regional averages.
- Dry cooling's possible water/electricity tradeoff is supported by research; its numerical size here is assumed.
- Model comparisons use exact coefficients: 87.5% lower emissions for the lower-carbon factor; 98.3% lower direct water and 12.5% higher facility electricity with dry cooling; 6.67% versus 2.00% total household energy burden when residents pay the upgrade.
- Facility electricity demand does not feed into household electricity prices in this model. Only the upgrade allocation changes the household surcharge.
- The Ohio tariff is described within its scope. The text does not assert that the Kasnik household is protected or that the reported crypto facility is exempt for a specific reason.
- HB 646 is described as a stalled effort reported in June 2026, not a claim about its present legal status.
